# Scenario Source Notes

These primary references ground the fictional scenarios for the Village of Sundarpur in authoritative, conservative emergency guidelines.

---

## 1. Earthquake: Village School (`school-earthquake`)
- **Situation:** Second-floor classroom shaking with swaying overhead lighting fixtures and shaking desks.
- **Guidance & Source:** 
  - **FEMA Earthquake Hazard Information Sheet** / [Ready.gov Earthquake Guidance](https://www.ready.gov/sites/default/files/2024-03/ready-gov_full-suite_hazard-info-sheets.pdf)
  - Key guideline: *Drop, Cover, and Hold On*.
- **Correct Choice Supported:**
  - `drop-cover-hold`: Drop down to knees to prevent falling, crawl beneath a sturdy desk to protect head and torso from falling lights/ceiling tiles, and hold on to the desk leg.
- **Incorrect Choices Grounded in Research:**
  - `run-stairwell`: Attempting to run during shaking is the primary source of falls, fractures, and severe lacerations from collapsing architectural components and broken windows.
  - `stand-doorway`: Doorways in modern structures do not offer superior structural protection over standard interior framing, and doors frequently slam violently during tremors, causing crush injuries.

## 2. Flood: River Crossing Road (`river-flood`)
- **Situation:** Monsoonal flash flood causing brown water of indeterminate depth to rush across a low-lying road.
- **Guidance & Source:** 
  - **FEMA Community Emergency Response Team (CERT) Hazard Annexes** / [FEMA Flood Guidance](https://www.ready.gov/sites/default/files/2019.CERT_.HazardAnnexes.PM_.FINAL_.508c.pdf)
  - NOAA / National Weather Service safety campaign: *"Turn Around Don't Drown!"*
- **Correct Choice Supported:**
  - `turn-around-high-ground`: Turning back immediately and moving toward the elevated ridge before waters rise further.
- **Incorrect Choices Grounded in Research:**
  - `wade-quickly`: As little as 6 inches of fast-moving water can knock an adult off their feet; washed-out culverts and downed electrical lines are completely masked by turbid floodwaters.
  - `drive-scooter`: Less than 12 inches of moving water can float or stall light motor vehicles; buoyant hydrodynamics easily carry two-wheelers and passenger vehicles downstream.

## 3. Fire: Village Home (`home-fire`)
- **Situation:** Late-night residential smoke alarm sounding with smoke creeping beneath a closed bedroom door.
- **Guidance & Source:** 
  - **U.S. Fire Administration (USFA)** / [USFA Home Fire Escape Plans](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prepare-for-fire/home-fire-escape-plans/)
  - NFPA Educational Messaging Advisory Committee recommendations.
- **Correct Choice Supported:**
  - `crawl-low-feel-door`: Toxic smoke and superheated combustion gases rise to the ceiling; breathable, cooler air is found in the lowest 12–24 inches. Testing the door surface and handle with the back of the hand prevents palm burns and indicates if high heat is immediately outside.
- **Incorrect Choices Grounded in Research:**
  - `grab-valuables-run`: Searching for laptops, phones, or papers delays evacuation; residential fires can double in size every 30 to 60 seconds, and breathing toxic smoke causes rapid disorientation.
  - `hide-in-closet`: Hiding cuts off egress routes, conceals occupants from search-and-rescue teams, and does not stop deadly carbon monoxide or cyanide smoke from infiltrating.
